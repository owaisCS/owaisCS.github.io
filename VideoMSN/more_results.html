Supplementary Results

Dataset Details

VideoMSN is evaluated on four standard action-recognition benchmarks. Pretraining and fine-tuning use unlabeled videos plus labels only at the fine-tuning stage.

▷ Kinetics-400 (Public link)

Kinetics-400 contains 306,245 YouTube clips across 400 action classes, with at least 400 clips per class and an average duration of about 10 seconds. Actions cover person actions, person–person interactions, and person–object interactions. It is the main large-scale pretraining and evaluation set in the paper.

▷ UCF-101 (Public link)

UCF-101 has 13,320 unconstrained YouTube clips from 101 human action categories, grouped into human–object interaction, body-motion only, human–human interaction, playing musical instruments, and sports. Videos are 320×240 at 25 fps with an average length of about 7.2 seconds.

▷ HMDB-51 (Public link)

HMDB-51 contains 6,766 clips from 51 action categories, with at least 101 clips per class, sourced from movies, public databases, and YouTube. Clips are standardized to 30 fps with height 240. Categories include facial actions, body movements, and human interactions.

▷ Something-Something V2 (Public link)

SSV2 has over 220K videos and 174 classes focused on fine-grained human–object interactions such as “turning something upside down.” It is motion-heavy and is the most challenging transfer setting for our 2D super-image formulation.

Tube Masking and Prototypes

These ablations use the full Kinetics-400 training set with VideoMSN-DeiT (ViT-S) and 16-frame inputs.

Tube masking vs random masking

Masking strategyTop-1
Random masking79.0
Tube masking80.0

Tube masking applies the same spatial mask to every frame in the super image. Because neighboring frames are highly correlated, independent per-frame masks leak information and let the model copy visible patches across time. Consistent tubes close that shortcut and improve top-1 accuracy by 1.0%.

Number of prototypes

# PrototypesTop-1

204880.0

Increasing the prototype count from 512 to 1024 yields a 1.4% gain. Doubling again to 2048 does not help, so we follow MSN and use 1024 prototypes of dimension 256.

SSV2 is motion-centric, so we retune a few design choices with VideoMSN-DINO (ViT-S).

Implementation Notes

  • Backbones: ViT-S and ViT-B, initialized from DeiT-v3 or distilled DINO-v3 image checkpoints.
  • Pretraining: AdamW, cosine learning rate, 50 epochs with 5-epoch warmup for VideoMSN-DeiT, 10 epochs with 1-epoch warmup for VideoMSN-DINO.
  • Regularization: DropPath 0.01, weight decay 0.01, prototype temperatures \(\tau=0.1\), \(\tau^+=0.025\), ME-MAX weight \(\lambda=5.0\).
  • Hardware: 4×NVIDIA H100 GPUs. Wall-clock comparisons with VideoMAE use the same server, 4×H100 80GB, batch size 32, 24 workers, Intel Xeon Platinum 8480C (112 cores), and 2 TB RAM.
  • Main-table Kinetics-400 numbers are averaged over two random seeds. Ablations and supplementary tables use a single seed to save compute.
